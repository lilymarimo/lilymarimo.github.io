Evening, love. How are you today? I hope you are doing good.<br>
<br>
Perhaps because I am in a holiday vibe, I am just very lazy lately. I was planning to study Japanese more during the holiday, but I sleep more instead. Haha.<br>
<br>
Honestly, today I would like to tell you bedtime stories about my past. I think you should know why I ended things with someone in the past, despite the long history we had.<br>
<br>
We were not really in a relationship. When we were about twenty or twenty one, we got close for about a month. As immature as I was back then, I ghosted him. It wasn't because he did something wrong, but because I felt suffocated and I did not know why, at the time. As it was approaching Ramadan as well, I decided to just flee. I wasn't sure how I ended it though, my memory is hazy around those period because clearly my attention was somewhere else.<br>
<br>
Throughout the time he was pursuing me, I was indifferent. Honestly, it was because I liked someone else and the crush was just too huge at the time, that I wasn't sure of this person who was pursuing me.<br>
<br>
This is going to be long, so I would cut it. For four years, he wished me birthdays every year. He kept showing up, without me asking. I was trying my best to not lead him on. I replied every text coldly and cut them short. Somehow, there were events when we met each other out of coincidences. First, it was a train to Bandung. Honestly, when I was about to leave Bandung, I asked him to see me in a restaurant one last time, to say goodbye. I don't remember whether I was clear enough back then.<br>
<br>
The next meeting was during the same flight we coincidentally shared to Tokyo, at the beginning of fall semester in 2024. We reconnected again. At the time, I was so caught up in my mental health issues that I didn't really care about him. However, perhaps, he felt it differently. He asked to meet in Tokyo, but I rejected it. Around December that year, I went to see a muslim therapist.<br>
<br>
After that, I began my healing journey, starting with Mom, and then him. I asked him to let me go after telling him that I have issues involving my childhood and my parents. It was a betrayal from Dad to Mom. We exchanged letters and ended it on good terms. At this point, I learned that we share completely different fundamental beliefs. He wasn't ready for a marriage either.<br>
<br>
Moving forward was easy back then because I was throwing myself at work. I just started my internship at a startup around June. Later I know that it was rough on him, but eventually he did move forward as well. He was with another person as well, a month after I asked him to let me go.<br>
<br>
It was October, I was hiking in Hakuba with Zahrah. He wished me birthday again, casually. I didn't realize it then, but it was like him dropping a bomb in my day of the year. I was a bit sad, furious, and happy. As it turned out, his relationship with another person didn't work out. His intention of wishing me birthday was also harmless perhaps, because we shared history in the past. Little did I know, it was the least thing I want to receive on my birthday.<br>
<br>
So I replied it coldly, without opening follow-up conversations whatsoever. After that, during Eid, he did the same thing, again. Casually dropping the bomb in my favorite time of the year. I replied coldly again, without follow-ups.<br>
<br>
At that point, I felt something needs to be clarified, either he ended it or I cut it off. So I traveled to his place to seek clarity. When I saw him, I knew right then that I was in love. My nervous system was alright after meeting him, which was very unusual because for five years I had felt choking sensation after our meetings.<br>
<br>
After our meeting, we knew that we had feelings for each other, still. We tried to make it work. I asked a lot of questions to him because my wound was triggered. Remember that he was with another person a month after I asked him to let me go? Yeah, I did not expect it to be that fast, although he kept coming back to me later.<br>
<br>
The more we exchanged information, the more we realize that the fundamental difference stays the same. I might have been better at recognizing my wounds and he might have been more ready, but we do not want the same thing in a marriage.<br>
<br>
After several phone calls, where I honestly couldn't fully convey the harder truths, we exchanged pages of letters. We hurt each other terribly in the process, but nothing we said was untrue. We finally ended it, after five years.<br>
<br>
My love, I am never looking for a perfection. I need a shared belief, much more than I realized. I also realized that the trust he earned through his way of showing up was demolished the second I knew about another woman. It is easy to be faithful when someone doesn't have an option. It was very hard for me because I deliberately chose him, with his consistency. Due to my history, I am quite immune to men's sweet words as well, they just bore me.<br>
<br>
I am grateful that I got to know him, and through him, I could see how Dad loves me better as well. However, my love, I just realized what it means to outgrow someone.<br>
<br>
I don't know why I wanted to share this with you, but I guess I was reminding myself why I shouldn't get stuck in this place. Because I already knew what I wanted, I already knew we couldn't be together. Now I am accepting the reality of it. The person you meet is someone who went through this, and the person you meet won't be able to be where she is now without experiencing those moments.<br>
<br>
I have been joking with Zahrah that I would just take PPE at Oxford, or co-parent a daughter, or volunteer in Africa and Middle East, or enroll in astronaut education program, if I won't get married.<br>
<br>
If we do meet in real life, be brave, my love, because I would rather be on my own rather than being with someone fearful.<br>
<br>
Ask me hard questions, challenge my perspectives, and eat one third of my meal.<br>
<br>
